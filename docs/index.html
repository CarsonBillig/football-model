Glossary

Spread
Points the favorite must win by. KC −3.5 means KC must win by 4 or more; the other side covers if it loses by 3 or less, or wins.
Moneyline
A bet on who wins outright. −150 means risk $150 to win $100; +130 means risk $100 to win $130.
Total (over/under)
A bet on whether both teams combined score more or fewer points than the line.
Juice / vig
The sportsbook's cut. At the standard −110 you risk $110 to win $100.
Break-even 52.4%
Because of the juice, you must win 52.4% of −110 bets just to break even.
Expected value (EV)
Average profit per $1 bet if the model's probability is right. +2% means 2 cents per dollar over the long run.
Stake
Suggested bet size as a share of your bankroll: a quarter of the Kelly formula, capped at 3%.
Opening / current / closing line
The first number posted, the number now, and the final number at kickoff. Lines move as money and news come in.
CLV (closing line value)
How much better your number was than the closing line. Consistently positive CLV is the best early sign of a real edge.
Push
The result lands exactly on the line, such as a 3-point win at −3. The bet is refunded.
Key numbers
NFL games end by 3 or 7 far more often than other margins, so a half point around 3 or 7 is worth far more than elsewhere.
Backtest
Replaying past seasons week by week, using only information available before each game, to see how the model would have done.

How the model works

Projection. A ridge regression on opponent-adjusted EPA, success rate, QB rating and rest, plus two power ratings (one from final scores, one from past betting lines), retrained every week on every completed game.

Respecting the market. NFL closing lines are very efficient, so in Market-adjusted view the model moves off the sportsbook's number only by the share its disagreements have earned in testing (currently 6% for spreads). That share is re-estimated every week from the backtest and every graded pick, so the model gains or loses trust depending on how it actually does.

Probabilities come from a score distribution that knows about key numbers, calibrated so that 70% picks win about 70% of the time.

Honest scorekeeping. In testing, market-adjusted spread picks hit 51.5% and model-only picks 51.1%; 52.4% is needed to profit. Picks are logged before kickoff and never changed afterwards.

Glossary

Spread
Points the favorite must win by. KC −3.5 means KC must win by 4 or more; the other side covers if it loses by 3 or less, or wins.
Moneyline
A bet on who wins outright. −150 means risk $150 to win $100; +130 means risk $100 to win $130.
Total (over/under)
A bet on whether both teams combined score more or fewer points than the line.
Juice / vig
The sportsbook's cut. At the standard −110 you risk $110 to win $100.
Break-even 52.4%
Because of the juice, you must win 52.4% of −110 bets just to break even.
Expected value (EV)
Average profit per $1 bet if the model's probability is right. +2% means 2 cents per dollar over the long run.
Stake
Suggested bet size as a share of your bankroll: a quarter of the Kelly formula, capped at 3%.
Opening / current / closing line
The first number posted, the number now, and the final number at kickoff. Lines move as money and news come in.
CLV (closing line value)
How much better your number was than the closing line. Consistently positive CLV is the best early sign of a real edge.
Push
The result lands exactly on the line, such as a 3-point win at −3. The bet is refunded.
Key numbers
Games end by 3 or 7 more often than other margins (less sharply in college than the NFL), so half points near them matter more.
Backtest
Replaying past seasons week by week, using only information available before each game, to see how the model would have done.

How the model works

Projection. A ridge regression on power ratings built from final scores, past betting lines and per-play efficiency (PPA and success rate), plus roster talent and returning production that matter most early in the season. All FCS opponents share one rating. Retrained every week on every completed game.

Respecting the market. College closing lines are efficient, though less than the NFL's, so in Market-adjusted view the model moves off the sportsbook's number only by the share its disagreements have earned in testing (currently 5% for spreads). That share is re-estimated every week from the backtest and every graded pick, so the model gains or loses trust depending on how it actually does.

Probabilities come from a score distribution that knows about key numbers, calibrated so that 70% picks win about 70% of the time.

Honest scorekeeping. In testing, market-adjusted spread picks hit 50.2% and model-only picks 50.3%; 52.4% is needed to profit. Picks are logged before kickoff and never changed afterwards.
